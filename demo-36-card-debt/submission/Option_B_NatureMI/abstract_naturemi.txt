Open-weight machine learning models distributed via public repositories increasingly underpin critical artificial intelligence systems. Model cards serve as the primary public transparency mechanism communicating behavioral boundaries, biases, and safety evaluations. Here, we conduct a comprehensive empirical evaluation of safety disclosure across 809,703 model cards and 48,247 supply-chain lineage edges on the Hugging Face Hub, encompassing 2.47 billion cumulative downloads. Contrary to the assumption that widely adopted models carry higher documentation standards, we find that the download-weighted popularity gap is falsified on the registered estimator (-2.9 percentage points). On the unregistered row-level vector, the download-weighted safety rate (38.40%) exceeds the unweighted rate (27.19%) by 11.21 points, but the 95% bootstrap interval ([-1.67, +24.68] points) spans both confirmation and falsification boundaries. Extreme Value Theory reveals that model downloads follow an infinite-variance Pareto distribution (Hill tail index alpha = 0.98 <= 2), rendering global weighted safety metrics hypersensitive to individual corporate releases: omitting the single most popular model shifts the global rate by 7.20 percentage points. Furthermore, derivative lineages exhibit severe documentation decay, with an 83.46% license omission rate among model merges. These findings demonstrate that unadjusted download weighting yields fragile governance indicators, highlighting the need for tail-trimmed estimators and structural provenance tracking.
