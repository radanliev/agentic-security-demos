\begin{abstract}
This paper is submitted to the Responsible Web track. Model cards serve as the primary accountability documents published with open-weight models on public Web registries. A central question for the Responsible Web is whether models that circulate widely and power downstream Web applications carry higher standards of safety documentation than the background repository population. This study investigates safety disclosure across 809,703 model cards and 48,247 supply-chain derivation edges on the Hugging Face Hub, accounting for $2.47 \times 10^9$ cumulative downloads. Evaluating a retrospectively registered popularity hypothesis (confirmed iff the gap reaches $+15$ points; falsified below $+10$ points), the registered binned estimator yields $-2.9$ points and is therefore falsified. The unregistered row-level estimate places the download-weighted rate at 38.40\% against 27.19\% unweighted (gap $+11.21$ points), with a bootstrap (1,000 resamples) 95\% interval of [$-1.67$, $+24.68$] points that covers both boundaries and is indeterminate. Hill estimates from Extreme Value Theory (EVT) indicate that public model downloads are consistent with an infinite-variance Pareto regime (Hill tail index $\alpha = 0.98 \le 2$), where classical Central Limit convergence breaks down. The weighted safety rate is driven by extreme leverage from a handful of corporate models: omitting the single most popular model alone swings the global weighted safety rate by 7.20 percentage points, and trimming the top ten models compresses the popularity gap to just $+1.34$ percentage points. Furthermore, lineage analysis across derivative models reveals severe documentation decay, with an 83.46\% licence omission rate among model merges. These findings indicate that unadjusted download weighting yields statistically brittle accountability metrics on the Web, highlighting the necessity of tail-trimmed indicators and supply-chain provenance tracking for responsible Web AI governance.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textit{\textbf{Web research question (Responsible Web).} When public Web registries distribute open-weight models that downstream Web applications integrate into search, dialogue, and client-side systems, does download-weighted safety disclosure exceed unweighted baseline rates by a registered margin of 15 percentage points? Across $2.47 \times 10^9$ downloads and 809,703 models, the registered binned estimator is falsified at $-2.9$ points, and the unregistered row-level estimand cannot confirm the margin.}

\section{Introduction}
Modern Web applications routinely integrate open-weight machine learning models retrieved from public Web repositories such as the Hugging Face Hub. Because integration delegates safety guardrails to downstream developers, model cards serve as primary accountability instruments communicating limitations, biases, and safety assessments.

\section{Methodology and Four-Level Safety Construct}
We construct a four-level ordinal scale ($L_0$ Absent, $L_1$ Boilerplate, $L_2$ Qualitative, $L_3$ Quantitative) across 809,703 model cards and trace 48,247 supply-chain lineage edges. Automated extraction is validated against human annotations ($\kappa = 0.81$).

\section{Registered Hypotheses and Empirical Findings}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{H1 (Temporal Divergence):} Falsified (odds ratio 0.79, $p = 0.0001$).
    \item \textbf{H2 (Popularity Dividend):} Falsified on binned estimator ($-2.9$ points); unregistered row-level gap ($+11.21$ points) bootstrap 95\% CI [$-1.67$, $+24.68$] points is indeterminate.
    \item \textbf{H3 (Lineage Decay):} Confirmed on licence omission (83.46\% on merges; 44.21\% overall).
\end{itemize}

\section{Extreme Value Theory and Super-Spreader Distortion}
Hill tail estimation ($\alpha = 0.98 \le 2$) places public model downloads in an infinite-variance Pareto regime. Omitting the top model alters global weighted safety by 7.20 percentage points, and trimming the top ten models reduces the popularity gap to $+1.34$ points.

\section{Conclusion and Governance Implications}
Unadjusted download weighting yields fragile Web accountability metrics. We recommend tail-trimmed estimators and mandatory lineage tracking for Responsible Web governance.
