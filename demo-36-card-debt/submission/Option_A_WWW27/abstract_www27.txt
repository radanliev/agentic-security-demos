Model cards serve as primary accountability documents published with open-weight models on public Web registries. A central question for the Responsible Web is whether models powering downstream Web applications carry higher standards of safety documentation than the background population. This study investigates safety disclosure across 809,703 model cards and 48,247 supply-chain derivation edges on Hugging Face Hub, accounting for 2.47 x 10^9 downloads. Evaluating a pre-registered popularity hypothesis (confirmed if the gap reaches +15 points; falsified below +10 points), the registered binned estimator yields -2.9 points and is falsified. The unregistered row-level estimate places download-weighted safety at 38.40% against 27.19% unweighted (gap +11.21 points), with a 95% bootstrap interval of [-1.67, +24.68] points spanning both boundaries. Extreme Value Theory indicates model downloads follow an infinite-variance Pareto regime (Hill tail index alpha = 0.98 <= 2), breaking central limit convergence. Omitting the single most popular model alone swings the weighted safety rate by 7.20 points, and trimming top ten models compresses the gap to +1.34 points. Furthermore, derivative lineages exhibit severe documentation decay, including an 83.46% licence omission rate on merges. Unadjusted download weighting yields fragile Web accountability metrics, requiring tail-trimmed estimators and provenance tracking.
