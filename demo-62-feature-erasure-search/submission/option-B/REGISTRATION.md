**Title:** Minimum-Cost Feature Erasure Search for Linear Classifiers

**Abstract (DRAFT):** A linear threshold classifier assigns a label from a weighted sum of input features. This article studies the discrete search problem of finding a minimum-cost set of feature erasures that flips the sign of that sum, and gives a complete enumeration baseline together with a greedy ranking by signed contribution. The search formulation is an artificial-intelligence method: states, actions, path cost and a comparison against an uninformed baseline. Public tabular benchmarks whose licences permit research use, including the UCI Adult census-income table, are identified as evaluation substrates for a later confirmatory study. A companion teaching demonstration runs the same search on eight labelled synthetic instances with at most eight features. No claim is made here about the robustness of any deployed model.

**Keywords:** heuristic search, linear classifiers, feature erasure, admissible heuristics, explainable AI
