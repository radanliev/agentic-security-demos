**Title:** Minimum Horn-Clause Deletion to Restore Consistency

**Abstract (DRAFT):** A Horn knowledge base is inconsistent when forward chaining derives false. This article studies the computational problem of deleting as few clauses as possible so that false is no longer derived, and gives a complete enumeration of deletion sets on small theories together with the forward-chaining closure used to test consistency. The contribution is a knowledge-representation and reasoning procedure with a clearly stated objective, not an application of a conventional solver to a new domain story. Later confirmatory theories would be taken from publicly licensed rule benchmarks once each licence has been checked. A companion teaching demonstration repairs eight labelled synthetic Horn theories. No complexity claim beyond the finite enumeration on those theories is made here.

**Keywords:** Horn clauses, belief revision, knowledge representation, consistency restoration, automated reasoning
